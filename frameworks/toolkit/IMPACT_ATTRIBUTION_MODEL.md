

# IMPACT ATTRIBUTION MODEL
EraIn AI — Proof Over Promises Framework

Version: 1.0
Status: Core Toolkit Component
Applies To: All Industries

---

## 1. Purpose

The Impact Attribution Model ensures that every intervention, recommendation, workflow, or governance improvement delivered by EraIn can be:

• Measured  
• Attributed  
• Quantified  
• Sustained  

If impact cannot be proven, it is not considered complete.

---

## 2. Core Principle

We do NOT claim improvement.

We prove:

Baseline → Intervention → Measured Change → Sustained Impact

Every client engagement must follow this loop.

---

## 3. Attribution Architecture

### Stage 1 — Baseline Establishment

Before any intervention:

1. Capture current state metrics (minimum 30–90 day history if available)
2. Identify:
   - Financial baseline (₹ / $)
   - Operational baseline
   - Time/cycle baseline
   - Error/rework baseline
3. Document:
   - Ownership
   - Data source
   - Frequency
   - Reliability confidence score

No baseline = No implementation.

---

### Stage 2 — Intervention Mapping

For every action:

Document:

• What changed  
• Why it changed  
• Who owns it  
• When it started  
• What metric it is expected to influence  

Every action must map to at least one measurable KPI.

---

### Stage 3 — Controlled Measurement

We compare:

Pre-intervention period  
vs  
Post-intervention period  

Using:

• Same measurement window
• Same data source
• Same definitions

Adjustments required if:
- Seasonality exists
- Demand volatility exists
- External event impact exists

---

### Stage 4 — Financial Translation Layer

Operational change must translate to financial impact.

Examples:

Downtime reduction → Increased output → Revenue gain  
Vendor price discipline → Procurement savings → Margin increase  
Shift discipline → Rework reduction → Cost avoidance  
Lead time reduction → Working capital release  

All improvements must convert into one of:

• Revenue increase
• Cost reduction
• Cost avoidance
• Working capital improvement
• Risk reduction (quantified probability-based)

---

### Stage 5 — Sustainability Check

Impact is only valid if:

• It sustains for 2–3 cycles minimum
• It does not degrade without monitoring
• Ownership remains active

If impact disappears after monitoring stops, it was control-dependent, not structural.

---

## 4. Attribution Categories

Every impact must be classified under one of:

### A. Direct Financial Gain
Immediate revenue or cost improvement.

### B. Avoided Loss
Prevention of penalty, churn, scrap, compliance breach.

### C. Efficiency Gain
Cycle time, throughput, utilization improvement.

### D. Governance Strengthening
Reduced risk probability (requires quantified risk scoring).

---

## 5. Impact Confidence Score

Each impact must be tagged:

High Confidence (Data-backed, direct linkage)
Medium Confidence (Strong correlation)
Low Confidence (Early-stage directional signal)

We only publicly report High Confidence impacts.

---

## 6. Impact Ledger Structure

Each client must have an Impact Ledger containing:

| Intervention | Metric Affected | Baseline | New Value | Financial Impact | Owner | Confidence | Status |
|-------------|----------------|----------|----------|------------------|-------|------------|--------|

The ledger is the single source of truth for ROI claims.

---

## 7. Anti-Fraud & Integrity Rules

Impact cannot be claimed if:

• Data source changed mid-cycle
• Definition of KPI changed without documentation
• Measurement window manipulated
• Attribution overlaps multiple uncontrolled changes

EraIn must protect credibility above optics.

---

## 8. Client Reporting Template

Quarterly Impact Summary:

1. Total Value Recovered (₹ / $)
2. Sustained Monthly Impact
3. Risk Reduction Score
4. Governance Maturity Level
5. Open Leakages Remaining

This becomes CXO-facing documentation.

---

## 9. Internal Rule

No marketing claim can be made without:

• Impact Ledger reference
• Confidence tag
• Measurement window defined

This protects EraIn’s 50-year credibility.

---

## 10. Completion Criteria

The Impact Attribution Model is considered operational when:

• Baseline library templates exist
• Ledger template exists
• Reporting format exists
• Financial translation model exists
• Governance sustainability rule exists

Once complete, this becomes mandatory for every engagement.
