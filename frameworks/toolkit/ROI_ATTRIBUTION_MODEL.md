

# ROI Attribution Model
## EraIn Impact Measurement System

---

# 1. Purpose

The ROI Attribution Model ensures every EraIn engagement:

• Quantifies financial impact
• Links outcomes to specific actions
• Separates signal from noise
• Proves value with defensible math
• Builds leadership confidence

This model is mandatory for:
- Pro Audit
- OpsMind
- Impact AI

No engagement moves forward without measurable attribution.

---

# 2. Core Principle

ROI must answer 5 questions:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. Who executed the change?
4. How much financial impact did it create?
5. Is the impact sustained?

If any of these are unclear → ROI is not valid.

---

# 3. Attribution Categories

All measurable impact falls into one of four categories:

## A. Cost Reduction

Examples:
- Scrap reduction
- Downtime reduction
- Vendor pricing correction
- Logistics penalty reduction
- Overtime optimization

Formula:

Impact = (Baseline Cost − Current Cost) × Volume

---

## B. Revenue Recovery

Examples:
- Reduced churn
- Increased throughput
- Pricing correction
- Improved conversion

Formula:

Impact = (Incremental Revenue − Incremental Cost)

---

## C. Capital Efficiency

Examples:
- Inventory reduction
- Working capital improvement
- Faster receivables
- Asset utilization improvement

Formula:

Impact = Capital Released × Cost of Capital

---

## D. Risk Avoidance

Examples:
- Compliance penalties avoided
- SLA breach reduction
- Safety incidents prevented
- Warranty claims reduction

Formula:

Impact = Probability × Potential Loss

---

# 4. Baseline Discipline

Before intervention:

• Define baseline window (typically 4–12 weeks)
• Normalize for seasonality
• Remove one-time anomalies
• Document data source
• Lock baseline version

Baseline must be:

Documented
Timestamped
Approved

No baseline → No ROI claim.

---

# 5. Attribution Levels

## Level 1 — Direct Attribution

Change directly caused by:
- EraIn recommendation
- Confirmed execution
- Isolated variable

Confidence: High

---

## Level 2 — Contributory Attribution

Multiple variables changed.
EraIn influence confirmed but not isolated.

Confidence: Medium

---

## Level 3 — Correlated Improvement

Improvement observed.
Causality unclear.

Confidence: Low

Level 3 impact is reported separately.
Never blended with Level 1.

---

# 6. Impact Tracking Window

Every improvement must track:

• Immediate impact (0–30 days)
• Stabilized impact (30–90 days)
• Sustained impact (90+ days)

True ROI is measured only after stabilization.

---

# 7. Compounding Effect Model

Some improvements create second-order effects:

Example:
Downtime ↓ → Throughput ↑ → Revenue ↑ → Overtime ↓

Primary Impact = Direct improvement
Secondary Impact = Downstream financial effect

We separate both in reporting.

---

# 8. Confidence Scoring

Each impact claim includes:

• Financial magnitude
• Attribution level
• Data quality score (1–5)
• Sustainability confidence (1–5)

This prevents inflated ROI narratives.

---

# 9. Executive Reporting Format

Every engagement produces:

1. Total Attributed Impact (₹ / $)
2. Impact by category
3. Top 3 drivers
4. Sustainability risk
5. Next compounding opportunity

Presented in:
- Executive summary
- Board-ready format
- Audit-traceable appendix

---

# 10. Anti-Fraud Safeguards

We do NOT:

• Claim impact without baseline
• Double-count improvements
• Blend Level 1 and Level 3 impact
• Ignore external factors
• Inflate projections

Trust > Marketing.

# 15. Statistical Validation Layer

To prevent false attribution, improvements must pass at least one validation method:

• Pre/Post Mean Comparison (minimum 4-week window)
• Rolling 8–12 week moving average comparison
• Control group comparison (if multi-site)
• Regression adjustment (for seasonality / macro impact)

Minimum data rule:
No impact claim under 4 weeks of stable data unless explicitly marked “Provisional”.

---

# 16. Seasonality & External Factor Adjustment

Before finalizing attribution, evaluate:

• Seasonality index
• Demand fluctuation
• Raw material price change
• Macro environment shifts
• Policy/regulatory impact

If external factor >20% influence → downgrade attribution level by one tier.

---

# 17. Attribution Governance Workflow

Every ROI claim must be:

1. Calculated by Analyst
2. Reviewed by Engagement Lead
3. Validated by Finance Owner
4. Signed-off by Client Sponsor

No executive summary includes unsigned ROI numbers.

---

# 18. ROI Formula Library

## Manufacturing Throughput

Impact = (New Output − Baseline Output) × Contribution Margin

## Service Utilization

Impact = (Billable Hours Increase × Avg Realization Rate)

## Inventory Reduction

Impact = (Inventory Reduced × Cost of Capital %) / 12

## Churn Reduction

Impact = (Churn Reduction % × Customer Base × Avg LTV)

## SLA Improvement

Impact = (Penalty Reduction + Retained Revenue)

---

# 19. Projection Discipline

Projected ROI must:

• Be labeled separately from realized ROI
• Include assumption list
• Include sensitivity band (+/- 15%)
• Include worst-case and best-case scenario

We never mix realized and projected numbers.

---

# 20. Compounding Index

We calculate a Compounding Index:

CI = (Sustained Impact × Confidence Score) ÷ Volatility Index

Where:
• Sustained Impact = 90+ day verified benefit
• Confidence Score = Weighted attribution × data quality
• Volatility Index = Variance in last 8 weeks

Higher CI = structurally embedded improvement.

---

# 21. ROI Dashboard Requirements

Every active client must have:

• Realized ROI (Level 1 only)

• Sustainability score
• Compounding index
• Owner accountability tracker

If traceability breaks → impact claim invalid.
